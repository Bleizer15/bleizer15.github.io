Are you the whole book?

The archive's own killer line: "an empty book also means every trade that happens, happens against us." The 25% cap is on reward score, which says nothing about depth. So depth was measured directly at a $50k book across 24 markets, stakes 5× to 102× the market minimum.

Measured  5–8% of resting depth inside the reward band, weighted; 21% in the thinnest market held. Because the strategy rests at the best reachable price, where score-per-share is highest, it reaches the score cap while holding a far smaller fraction of actual size. The empty-book failure mode does not apply at this scale. It is the one objection in the archive that this work clears outright.

The engineering

Three diversification designs were built. How the first two failed produced the third.

Correlation clusters

  • Chaining joined LoL Worlds to Musk tweet count at a true ρ of −0.017

The shipped guard is pairwise and merges nothing, which is what stops the chaining failure. It combines several independent signals, any one of which is enough to refuse a market, and it caught every pair the two earlier designs missed. Measured It holds zero related pairs where the guard it replaces held one, and allocates roughly twice the capital. Strictly safer and larger — the uncommon case where a change is both.

The exchange's own scoring rule is also implemented correctly here, where the original code used a simplification that quietly overstated our share. The competitor score still cannot be computed exactly from public data, because scoring is per maker and the order book carries no maker identity; the licensed material gives the provable bounds and works inside them rather than pretending to a point estimate.

It is the rotation, not the position

Measured  Competition against a reward pool rises sharply and predictably with the pool's age — corr(log age, log competing score) = +0.463 across the eligible universe, with the oldest pools carrying an order of magnitude more competition per dollar than the youngest. The archive records a single position decaying from $8.28/day to $1.99/day in 24 hours as makers arrived.

Supply is not the constraint: the platform replenishes faster than any book of this size can absorb. The consequence is operational. A bot that stops for a week does not hold its yield, so uptime is a product requirement rather than a nice-to-have. The age thresholds and the rotation rule are in the licensed material.

The acceptance test

Redesigned around the cost side, because that is what decides the sign.

  1. Week 1 — count fillsBuyer runs the selector in the shipped configuration at ~$20,000. Fill count alone separates the two cost regimes: ~1,048/day versus ~2.2/day. No ambiguity.
  2. Weeks 1–2 — measure markout30-second markout on every fill, against the break-even of −0.0028/share. The harness ships with the licence.
  3. Also verify reward share/rewards/user/percentages gives the exchange's own reported share per market. Median absolute error versus predicted share ≤ 25% over ≥ 10 market-days.
  4. SettleMonth one is refunded if measured net over 30 days is below zero — provided the shipped configuration was run: the supplied filter enabled, capital inside the stated band, uptime above 90%. The test runs alongside live operation, not instead of it: capital is deployed and earning from day one.
